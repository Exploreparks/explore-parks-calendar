  • Apply: Spring lottery on Recreation.gov

  • Trailhead: The Grotto, shuttle stop 6.

Mini brochure: the Angels Landing hike

  • The numbers: 5.4 miles round trip, 1,488 ft of climbing, about 4 hours for most people. Summit elevation is about 5,990 ft.
  • Where the name came from: Methodist minister Frederick Vining Fisher visited in 1916 and allegedly said "only an angel could land there."
  • Walter's Wiggles: 21 steep switchbacks, named for Walter Ruesch, the park building foreman who helped build the trail in the 1930s. It leads up to Scout Lookout.
  • Refrigerator Canyon: a cool, shaded flat stretch before the Wiggles. Welcome relief on the climb.
  • Scout Lookout needs no permit: it is 2.25 miles from the Grotto with about 1,000 ft of climbing and great views. A good stopping point if anyone in your group fears heights.
  • The chains: the last half mile follows a narrow ridge with a single chain for a handrail and drop-offs of up to 1,000 ft on each side. Permit required past Scout Lookout. Skip it if the rock is wet or icy, storms are near, or it will be dark before you finish.
  • Wildlife: look for ravens, squirrels, and chipmunks (never feed them). California condors sometimes soar near Angels Landing. Keep your distance.
  • Before you go: fill water at the Grotto (the only drinkable water on the trail), use the restrooms there, wear broken-in boots, bring a headlamp, and screenshot your permit email because cell service is poor.
  • March tip: shaded sections can be icy, and the metal chains are cold, so bring gloves.

Mini brochure: more to do in Zion

  • West Rim Trail to Scout Lookout: the same climb without the chains, 3 to 4 hours, no permit.
  • Watchman Trail: 3.3 mi round trip, about 2 hours, 368 ft of climbing. It starts at the Visitor Center near Watchman Campground and ends at a view of the Towers of the Virgin and Springdale.
  • Canyon Overlook Trail: 1 mi round trip, 163 ft of climbing, a quick viewpoint above lower Zion Canyon. Parking is limited, east of the Zion tunnel.
  • Emerald Pools (Lower, Middle, Upper): waterfalls and pools reached from the Grotto or Zion Lodge area.
  • Riverside Walk and The Narrows: the Narrows hike is in the river, up to 8 hours, 9.4 mi. It can close for high water or flash floods, so check conditions first.
  • Pa'rus Trail: an easy paved trail beside the Virgin River, open to bikes, starting near South Campground.
  • Sand Bench Trail: follows the remains of an ancient landslide that dammed the Virgin River about 8,000 years ago and formed a lake that reached back to Angels Landing.
  • Zion Canyon Shuttle: free, no reservation needed, runs from the Visitor Center to the trailheads. Check the seasonal schedule.
